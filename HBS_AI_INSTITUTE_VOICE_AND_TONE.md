## Harvard Business School — the institutional mission underneath everything

- The stated mission: **"to educate leaders who make a difference in the
  world"** ([HBS: Our Mission](https://www.hbs.edu/about/mission)).
- Former dean Nitin Nohria's gloss on it: leaders need both *competence*
  (the general-manager perspective HBS cultivates) and *character* (the
  difference between being self-interested and self-centered) — and
  "making a difference" means people who **"create real value for
  society, and who create value before claiming value"**
  ([HBS Alumni Bulletin](https://www.alumni.hbs.edu/stories/Pages/story-bulletin.aspx?num=2148)).
- Stated values: an environment of **"trust and mutual respect, free
  expression and inquiry, and a commitment to truth, excellence, and
  lifelong learning"** ([HBS Community Values](https://www.hbs.edu/about/campus-and-culture/community-values)).

This is the register underneath the AI Institute's more provocative,
business-forward voice: an ethical/leadership framing sits under the
pragmatic one, even when a given page reads as pure business strategy.

## The HBS AI Institute's specific voice

Real, cited phrases — this is the clearest signal of how they actually
write:

- **Thesis-driven, slightly provocative opening claims.** The Institute
  is built on the premise that **"AI technology is only half the
  answer"** — businesses must also revamp their processes to harness its
  potential ([Institute Overview](https://aiinstitute.hbs.edu/institute-overview/)).
  Same pattern elsewhere: *"The Real AI Divide Isn't Who Uses It Most"*
  ([aiinstitute.hbs.edu](https://aiinstitute.hbs.edu/the-real-ai-divide-isnt-who-uses-it-most/)),
  *"Leaders: AI Adoption is Up to You"*
  ([aiinstitute.hbs.edu](https://aiinstitute.hbs.edu/leaders-ai-adoption-is-up-to-you/)).
  Headlines assert a reframed claim rather than describe a topic neutrally.
- **Triadic parallel verb structures.** Frontier Firms exist to
  **"transform operations, accelerate innovation, and amplify human
  capacity"** and to help organizations **"reimagine work patterns,
  reinvent operations, and generate new business models"**
  ([Frontier Firm AI Initiative](https://aiinstitute.hbs.edu/frontier-firm-ai-initiative/)).
  Three parallel verbs, escalating scope, no "and" until the last item —
  a recurring rhythm worth noticing.
- **Coined terms treated as defined concepts, capitalized like a proper
  noun.** "Frontier Firm" is formally defined: *"a human led, agent
  operated organization that puts AI at the core of their strategy..."*
  — deliberately hyphen-free ("human led," not "human-led"), stated as
  if introducing a term of art.
- **Research-credibility anchoring, constantly.** Learning content is
  described as *"created through cutting-edge research of Harvard
  Business School faculty on how AI is transforming business"*
  ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/digital-data-design-institute-at-harvard-officially-launches-future-proof-with-ai-upskilling-learning-program-302412635.html)),
  and initiatives are attributed by name to **Dean Srikant Datar**
  specifically — authority is personal and credentialed, not just
  institutional.
- **Short, punchy, benefit-forward program names.** *"Future Proof with
  AI"*, *"Frontier Firm AI Initiative"* — two to four words, a verb or a
  clear benefit, no subtitle needed to explain what it's for.
- **Occasional vivid/informal flourish inside a formal register.**
  *"corporate adventurers,"* *"inventing the future"* — the Institute
  will reach for a livelier phrase even in otherwise buttoned-up material,
  rather than staying uniformly dry.
- **Headline structure that implies a journey or transformation arc.**
  *"...Mapping the Journey from AI Promise to Enterprise Performance"*
  ([Morningstar/PR Newswire](https://www.morningstar.com/news/pr-newswire/20251118ne26791/the-digital-data-design-institute-at-harvard-and-microsoft-launch-accelerated-ai-research-initiative-mapping-the-journey-from-ai-promise-to-enterprise-performance))
  — "Promise" to "Performance" is a deliberate rhyme/echo, not accidental.

## The name transition worth knowing

The Institute was previously the **Digital, Data, and Design Institute
("D³")** and is now the **Harvard Business School AI Institute** — the
"D³" name persists in some URLs (`d3.harvard.edu`, `d3.sana.ai`) and in
the job posting's "D³ Institute" reference, which is the same
organization mid-rebrand, not a separate one (already noted in
`ROLE_CONTEXT.md`).

## The learning platform (D³/Sana, "Future Proof with AI")

Public information only — the platform itself is gated and I could not
access `d3.sana.ai/discover`'s actual content:

- Publicly launched as **"Future Proof with AI,"** an AI-enabled
  upskilling platform for mid-career professionals, built on Sana Labs'
  AI-native learning platform (Sana was acquired by Workday in late 2025;
  Sana Learn continues as a standalone product)
  ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/digital-data-design-institute-at-harvard-officially-launches-future-proof-with-ai-upskilling-learning-program-302412635.html);
  [Sana Labs](https://sanalabs.com/products/sana-learn/)).
- Modules span **marketing, strategy, finance, data science operations,
  R&D, and HR**, each tied explicitly back to named HBS faculty research.

## Practical guidance for the workforce

This isn't a house style the Directors are boxed into — it's a real
reference point for when a deliverable should sound like it could sit
next to the Institute's own material (an interview work sample, a
LinkedIn post about your own project, a pitch to a real HBS audience).
Draw on it selectively:

- **Doriot** (market intelligence) and **Copeland** (content production)
  are the two most likely to want this register — briefings and
  converted assets aimed at an executive HBS audience.
- The triadic-verb pattern and research-anchoring habit are easy,
  low-risk borrows for any Director's polish pass.
- The provocative-thesis headline pattern is higher-risk to imitate
  without real backing — only reach for it when there's an actual claim
  worth defending, not as a formula.
- Kaitlyn's own writing doesn't need to mimic this — it's context for
  what the team she'd be joining sounds like, not a bar she's being held
  to by this file.
